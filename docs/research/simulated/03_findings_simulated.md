## 2. Goals

### Goal 1 · Boredom / audio–length mismatch is real and unsolved → **disproved as stated (2–4 / 10)**
- **Bored at all:** 3/10 (P9, P10 strongly; P1 mildly at min 10–15). 5/10 explicitly not bored (P2, P4, P6, P7, P8).
- **Length mismatch happens:** 6/10 (P1, P2, P3, P5, P9, P10). But **5 of those 6 already have a workaround**: run for one episode (P9), pick a video by its length (P10), run until the chapter ends (P5), extend the run for the episode (P3), switch to music for the last 5 min (P2). Only P9's near-fall and P10's walk-to-finish read as real pain.
- **Surprise:** the two most bored people both said audio alone doesn't hold them ("I need to SEE stuff", P9; video only, P10). The audio-only listeners aren't bored. This is the biggest risk to the PRD's core user.

### Goal 2 · They care how audio ends when they stop early → **disproved (1–2 / 10)**
- 8/10 let it keep playing or forgot it entirely. Early stops are caused by bodies, kids, phones and weather, never by the audio.
- What hurts is **losing their place**, not the ending: P7 (5 min of rewinding), P5 (2 min, mild). Both are book listeners.
- Controls: earbud/watch tap 6/10 (P2, P3, P5, P6, P7, P8); phone 4/10 (P1, P4, P9, P10, all treadmill or casual).

### Goal 3 · Breaks lead to quitting; coming back is hard → **weakened (4 / 10)**
- Gap → drift or near-quit: P3, P4, P9, P10. Others "always come back" (P2, P5, P6, P7, P8).
- **Every comeback in those 4 (plus P1) was triggered by a person or a commitment** (sister, friend, coworker's race, partner's 5K, doctor's reading). **0/10 came back because of content.**
- Content resume after a gap: handled by apps (Spotify, Overcast, Audible) for 8/10; real pain only for P7 (library loan expired).

### Scary question · Tried something to make running less boring
- **5/10 spent time or money specifically on it:** P1 (Nike Run Club, quit "too peppy"), P3 (Zombies, Run!, "corny"), P5 (Audible ~$180/yr; Zombies, Run! 1 month, "same three beats"), P9 (Peacock, ClassPass ~$150, Peloton trial), P10 (Zombies, Run! 1 week; quit over sprint commands and outdoor assumption).
- **3/10 tried a story-running app and abandoned it.** Reasons were cheesy/repetitive story and being told to sprint, not "story audio is a bad idea".
- 3/10 tried nothing (P2, P6, P8).

## 4. Script problems found in the dry run
1. **Every interviewer slipped once**, which is an artifact: the agents were told to slip at most once. Still, all 10 slips were a "would you use / would it help" question, and at least 5 (P3, P5, P7, P8, P9) were about matching the audio to the run. The natural follow-up to Q6–Q7 leads straight into a pitch. Add a guard line: after Q7, ask "What did you do about it?" and stop.
2. **Q13 is hypothetical** ("would you rather touch your phone or press a button"). Rewrite as: "The last time you paused audio mid-run, what did you touch?"
3. **Goal 2 is framed around "how the audio ends"**, but answers kept pointing at "losing my place". Add: "The next time you pressed play, where did it start? What did you do?"
4. **No question covers video or treadmill screens.** Bored runners watch, not listen. Add to Block A: "Is there a screen in front of you when you run? What's on it?"
5. **Q17 doesn't ask what brought them back.** Comebacks were all social. Add: "Who or what got you out the door the first day back?"
6. P5's slip landed at min 56, before the commitment step. Keep the Block D → close transition scripted.

## 5. What surprised me / next 3 big questions (for real interviews)
1. **Is boredom an audio problem at all?** Real interviews: do bored treadmill runners ever choose audio-only, or always a screen? Kill line: if ≥3 of 5 real "bored" recruits need video, US-1 is aimed at the wrong user.
2. **Is "losing my place" the real early-stop pain?** Kill line: if <2 of 5 real interviewees describe rewinding or getting lost, drop US-4's "Finish now" choice and keep only save/resume.
3. **What did story-running apps get wrong?** Recruit 2–3 people who tried and quit Zombies, Run! or similar. Ask about the last session before they quit.

Real-recruit pointers from this run: re-use the screener; the intros above are fictional, so start from the waitlist *types*, not names.
