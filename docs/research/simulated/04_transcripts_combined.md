## 1. Brianna Cole

**Interviewer:** Hi, thanks so much for doing this. Quick context: I'm doing a class research project about how people run, and I have nothing to sell. I'm stuck on understanding how people plan a run, what they listen to, and what happens when a run doesn't go as planned. You run regularly, so you can really help. Could you walk me through a few specific runs? I'd like to record audio just to keep my notes accurate. Is that OK?

**Brianna:** Yeah, totally, record away. Although "run regularly" is, um, generous. Like, I'll tell you if something's dumb, but I'm also gonna tell you I'm not a real runner. I'm a person who runs sometimes.

**Interviewer:** That's exactly who I want to talk to.

**Brianna:** Okay good. Low bar. I can clear a low bar.

**Interviewer:** How did you get into running?

**Brianna:** Oh god. Okay. Honest version? I went through a breakup in, like, April, and then in May my sister's wedding photos came back and I was in all of them and I was like... hm. Not that I looked bad, I just looked *sad*. Like sad in a sundress. So I downloaded Couch to 5K. The free one, the British one? The NHS one.

**Interviewer:** What happened next?

**Brianna:** I did it! Which, shocking. It's like nine weeks but it took me ten, I repeated a week because I had a bad cold. There's this lady in your ear, she's like, "Right, you'll start running... now," and you just do what she says. I finished it in July. I remember the last one, the thirty-minute one, I did it on the Community Path and I didn't stop the whole time and I kind of wanted to cry? Like at a guy walking his dog. He didn't know.

**Interviewer:** How did that feel?

**Brianna:** Proudest I've been of my body in maybe ever. Which is a weird thing to say about thirty minutes of jogging. But yeah.

**Interviewer:** About how often do you run now?

**Brianna:** Less than I want. Like three times a month? I want it to be like twice a week. Last month I think it was three. Maybe that's generous too.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through your last run.

**Brianna:** Sunday. So, the 26th. I went out around 8:40, which for me on a Sunday is heroic. Community Path from my place, out toward Davis and back. Twenty-eight minutes, I think? I looked at my phone after. I walked twice, both on that little uphill near Cedar Street, which, if you know it, it's barely a hill. It's a suggestion of a hill. But it gets me every time.

**Interviewer:** How did you feel afterwards?

**Brianna:** Smug. For like an hour. I made eggs and I was very smug about the eggs. And then the smug wore off and I was just a person on a couch.

**Interviewer:** On Sunday, how did you decide how long to go?

**Brianna:** Um. I didn't really? Like, I knew I'd do the Davis loop because it's the one I know. I guess it ends up being about half an hour. I don't plan "I'm doing three miles." I'm not that person.

**Interviewer:** Is the length the same every time?

**Brianna:** No, it's — okay, on weekdays it's literally "how long until I have to shower for work." Like if I wake up at 6:15 and I have to be at the office at 7:45, I do the math backwards. So that's like twenty-five minutes. Or in the evening it's daylight, which, now it's dark at like five, so. That's a whole problem.

**Interviewer:** What made Sunday longer or shorter than usual?

**Brianna:** Longer-ish, because no shower deadline. But I didn't go *way* longer. I don't know, twenty-eight felt like enough. I'm not trying to be a hero.

**Interviewer:** What were you listening to on Sunday?

**Brianna:** Smartless, I think. Or — no, it was Call Her Daddy. Yeah, Call Her Daddy. Don't judge me.

**Interviewer:** No judgment. Why that?

**Brianna:** It's just what I listen to. It's what's next in my feed. I don't pick a running podcast, I just pick whatever podcast I'm on. Honestly the thing I miss is the Couch to 5K lady. Like, once that ended — and I know this is pathetic — I missed having someone telling me what to do. Podcasts don't tell you what to do. Alex Cooper is not gonna say "okay, walk for ninety seconds." She's busy.

**Interviewer:** Tell me more about missing that. When did you notice it?

**Brianna:** Like the first week after I finished. I went out and I was like, okay... now what? And I just ran until I felt like stopping, which was like eleven minutes. It's like being let off a leash and just standing there.

**Interviewer:** What did you do about it?

**Brianna:** I mean, I tried Nike Run Club, I'll get to that, it was bad. Otherwise nothing. I just kind of run now.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about a time what you were listening to didn't fit the run.

**Brianna:** Ha, okay, yes. So there was a Smartless episode — this was like September? — and it ended, like, eight minutes before I was done. And it went quiet and I was like, no, I can't do this in silence, I'll die. So I pulled my phone out while running and I'm scrolling, trying to find something, and I spent, like, forty seconds just scrolling, and I almost ate it on a root. Like a full stumble.

**Interviewer:** What happened next?

**Brianna:** I put on — I don't even remember. Some random episode. And then finished. It wasn't a big deal, I laughed about it. Mostly I was embarrassed in case somebody saw me.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen, the audio running out before you're done?

**Brianna:** That's the only time I really remember. Most episodes are, like, an hour, so they're longer than me. I'm usually the one who runs out.

**Interviewer:** Has audio ever kept going after you stopped? What did you do?

**Brianna:** All the time, but that's just — it keeps going. I don't care. I'll get to that too, I'm sure.

**Interviewer:** What was the hardest moment of your last run?

**Brianna:** Minutes ten to fifteen. Every single time. That's when my brain starts negotiating. Like, "Hey. You could walk. Who would know? Nobody's checking." And Sunday that's around the Cedar Street thing, so the hill and the negotiating kind of team up on me.

**Interviewer:** What got you through it on Sunday?

**Brianna:** Um... honestly I didn't get through it, I walked. Twice. And then I started again because I could see the end, like I know where the bend is. That's it. Nothing magic.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever tried to make running less boring? What did you try, and what did it cost you?

**Brianna:** Okay. So, I bought earbuds — Soundcore, they were like forty-five bucks — because my AirPods kept falling out when I ran. Like literally fell out onto the path once. So that's money. And I tried Nike Run Club, the guided runs, twice. Free. And I hated it.

**Interviewer:** Why?

**Brianna:** The coach was so peppy. It's like a Peloton person yelling at you. "You're doing AMAZING!" I'm not, sir. I'm walking. It felt fake. The Couch to 5K lady was kind of dry and British and that's why I believed her.

**Interviewer:** How much time did you spend on Nike Run Club?

**Brianna:** Two runs and then I deleted it. So like, an hour total, plus downloading it and making an account, which was annoying. I haven't paid for any apps. I'm not gonna pay for an app to tell me I'm amazing.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you stopped a run earlier than planned.

**Brianna:** Oh, that's easy, like the week before. The 19th, I think — it was a Sunday too. I got a side stitch, like a real stabby one, around minute fourteen. And I just... walked home. I didn't even try to push it.

**Interviewer:** How did that feel?

**Brianna:** Kind of annoyed? But also, like, my body said no. I'm not gonna argue with a stitch. I was more annoyed that it was in the negotiating window, because it felt like my brain won.

**Interviewer:** What was playing in your ears right then?

**Brianna:** A podcast. Smartless maybe? I don't remember, honestly.

**Interviewer:** What did you do with it?

**Brianna:** Nothing. I let it keep playing. Walked home with it on, went inside, and finished it while I was doing my hair. It's a podcast, it doesn't care that I stopped running.

**Interviewer:** So — and this is me thinking out loud — if your audio could kind of wrap itself up nicely when you stopped early, like end at a good spot, would you use that?

**Brianna:** Uh... I mean, sure? I guess? Is that a thing?

**Interviewer:** Sorry, I started talking about my own idea there. Back to what you were saying — when you walked home on the 19th, was there any moment where what was playing bugged you?

**Brianna:** No. Honestly, no. I really don't care how the audio ends. I didn't think about it for one second. If anything I was thinking about the stitch and whether I ate too close to running. Which I did. I had a bagel.

**Interviewer:** Has a podcast or audiobook ever stopped at a bad moment for you?

**Brianna:** Only the Smartless thing, where it ended early. That's the bad moment. Stopping early on *my* side, the podcast's fine. It's just a podcast.

**Interviewer:** When you're tired mid-run and want to change something, what do you actually do — phone or earbuds?

**Brianna:** Phone. Always phone. I don't even know what my earbuds do if you tap them. I think one tap is pause? Or maybe it's two? I'm scared of it. I tapped one once to fix it in my ear and it skipped something and I was like, nope.

**Interviewer:** Why phone?

**Brianna:** Because I can see it. I know what's happening. Even though, yeah, see: almost tripping. I didn't say it was a good system.

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you went several days without running.

**Brianna:** Early September. Like ten days? Maybe eleven. Our office was short-staffed, one of the hygienists left and they hadn't hired yet, so I was doing extra patients and staying late and I just — I was dead. I'd come home and lie on the floor.

**Interviewer:** How did you start again?

**Brianna:** Okay, so this is actually the real answer to everything. My coworker Dana came up to me — she's at the front desk — and she was like, "Do you want to do the Thanksgiving turkey trot with me?" And I said yes before I thought about it. And she sent me the link that night and I registered. Thirty-five dollars. And then I was like, oh no, now I have to run. So I went out that weekend.

**Interviewer:** Why did the registration matter?

**Brianna:** Because I paid for it and because Dana. Like, if it was just me I'd let myself off. But I'm not gonna walk the whole turkey trot next to Dana while she's jogging. I have pride. A little.

**Interviewer:** When you came back after those ten days, what did you do with whatever you'd been listening to?

**Brianna:** I just hit play on whatever was next. I don't think I even finished the old episode. Spotify remembers where I was, so I guess technically I could have, but I didn't care. I skipped to the new one.

**Interviewer:** Do you usually remember where you left off?

**Brianna:** No, the app does. Like it shows the little bar. I don't keep track at all. If it's a podcast it doesn't matter anyway, there's no plot.

**Interviewer:** Has missing a few runs ever made you stop for a long time?

**Brianna:** Not yet. The ten days is the longest. But I'm scared of December, honestly. Like after the turkey trot, what's the thing? There's no Dana thing after that. And it's dark and gross. So ask me in January.

**Interviewer:** What's the plan for after the turkey trot?

**Brianna:** There's no plan. That's what I'm telling you. I'll probably sign up for something else, if somebody makes me.

**Interviewer:** Besides the earbuds and Nike Run Club, have you paid for or spent time on anything else to keep running — a club, gear, a class?

**Brianna:** Shoes. I got real running shoes in June, like, a store guy watched me jog on a treadmill. That was like one-thirty? One-forty. That was during Couch to 5K. And the turkey trot, thirty-five. No club. Clubs scare me, everyone's in tiny shorts and has a watch.

**Interviewer:** When was the last time you spent anything?

**Brianna:** The turkey trot. September. That's the last running money.

**Interviewer:** What do you do now to get yourself out the door?

**Brianna:** Um. Honestly? I think about the turkey trot. And I lay my clothes out the night before, sometimes. Like on a weekday I put my sports bra on the chair so I'll feel guilty. That works maybe half the time.

**Interviewer:** When was the last time it worked?

**Brianna:** Sunday! I laid stuff out Saturday night. And it didn't work on Thursday, I just looked at it and got in the shower.

**Brianna:** Okay wait, so what's the actual thing? Like what are you making?

**Interviewer:** It's a running app with audio. That's honestly all it is right now.

**Brianna:** Okay. Not peppy, please.

**Interviewer:** Noted. Next week I'll have a short sample. Would you run with it once and give me 15 minutes of feedback?

**Brianna:** I mean... if you give me an actual day. If you say "whenever," I won't. I'm telling you that now. I will forget.

**Interviewer:** How about you run with it Sunday the 8th, and we talk Monday the 9th after work?

**Brianna:** Sunday the 8th, yeah, okay. If it's not raining. Okay, even if it's raining, fine. Monday after six, I'm done with patients by five-thirty.

**Interviewer:** Great. And is there a friend who runs that I could talk to? Could you introduce us?

**Brianna:** Dana! Dana Ruiz. The turkey trot one. She's way more into it than me, she has a watch and everything. I'll text her tonight and give her your email, but you have to promise not to make her run longer.

**Interviewer:** Promise. Thank you, this was really helpful.

**Brianna:** Was it? I feel like I just told you I'm lazy for an hour.
