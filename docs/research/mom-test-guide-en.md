# Customer Interview Guide (Mom Test) — Runners in the US

5 interviews × 60 minutes. Based on *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick). Goal: learn what runners actually do, from specific past events, not collect opinions or compliments.

## 1. The three rules
1. Talk about **their life**, not our idea. Do not mention the product until the final commitment step.
2. Ask about **specific moments in the past**, not generics or the future ("the last time…", not "would you…").
3. **Talk less, listen more.** Aim for under 30% of the talking.

## 2. Who to recruit (who-where pair)
**Who:** adults living in the United States who have run at least once a month over the last 3 months (outdoors or treadmill, any distance) and sometimes wear headphones while running.
**Where:** friends and friends-of-friends; campus or community gym and rec center; local run clubs and group runs; running groups on Meetup, Strava or Facebook.

**Must have**
- Lives in the US; comfortable having a full interview in English.
- Ran at least once a month on average in the last 3 months.
- Has worn headphones on at least some runs (music, podcast or audiobook).
- Can give 60 minutes and agrees to audio recording (notes only; not shared).

**Quota for 5 people (mix of relationship to running)**
| Slots | Type | Screening question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Beginner (started less than 6 months ago) | "When did you start running?" |
| 1 | On-and-off (a gap of 2+ weeks in the last 6 months) | "When was the last time you went 2+ weeks without running?" |
| 2 | Regular (2+ runs a week) | "How many times did you run last month?" |
| 1 | Finds running boring (self-described, or mostly treadmill) | "What do you usually do to get through a run?" |

**Exclude**
- People who have seen our documents, mockups or project details (including classmates).
- People building a similar product.
- People who do not run, or run only for a required test.
- Do not take all five from one friend circle; use at least three different groups.

**Picking friends:** prefer people who will say "that's not true for me" over people who agree with everything. At the end of each interview ask for one introduction to another runner (a warm intro for a later round).

## 3. Three things we want to learn
Decide these with your team before the first interview.
1. **How runners decide a run, and what they listen to** — is boredom or a mismatch between audio and run length a real, currently unsolved problem?
2. **What happens when they stop early** — do they care how the audio ends, and what do they do?
3. **What happens after a break** — how do they come back, and does missing runs lead them to quit?

**The scary question (ask it every time):** *"Have you ever tried to make running less boring? What did you try, and what did it cost you in time or money?"* If nobody has tried anything, the problem may not be painful enough.

## 4. 60-minute plan
| Min | Part | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Framing | Use the opening script below |
| 5–15 | Background | Their running history; walk through the last run |
| 15–30 | Block A | Deciding the run, listening, boredom (goal 1) |
| 30–42 | Block B | Stopping early (goal 2) |
| 42–52 | Block C | Breaks and coming back (goal 3) |
| 52–58 | Block D | What they have already tried; time and money spent |
| 58–60 | Close | Commitment asks (section 6), thanks, one introduction |

### Opening script (Vision · Framing · Weakness · Pedestal · Ask)
> I'm doing a class research project about how people run, and I have **nothing to sell**. I'm stuck on understanding how people plan a run, what they listen to, and what happens when a run doesn't go as planned. You run regularly, so you can really help. Could you walk me through a few specific runs? I'd like to record audio just to keep my notes accurate. Is that OK?

## 5. Questions
Always follow up with: "What happened next?", "Why?", "What did you do about it?", "How did that feel?"

**Background (5–15)**
1. How did you get into running? About how often do you run now?
2. Walk me through your **last** run: where, what time, how long, how did it feel afterwards?

**Block A — deciding, listening, boredom (15–30)**
3. In your last run, how did you decide how long to go? Was it planned or decided on the spot?
4. Is the length the same every time? What made your last run longer or shorter than usual?
5. What were you listening to on your last run? Why that?
6. Tell me about a time what you were listening to didn't fit the run.
7. Has your audio ended before you finished, or kept going after you stopped? What did you do?
8. What was the hardest moment of your last run? What got you through it?
9. **(Scary question)** Have you ever tried to make running less boring? What did you try, and what did it cost you?

**Block B — stopping early (30–42)**
10. Tell me about the **last time** you stopped a run earlier than planned. What happened?
11. What was playing in your ears at that moment? What did you do with it?
12. Has a podcast, audiobook or show ever stopped at a bad moment? What happened, and what did you do next?
13. When you are tired, would you rather touch your phone or press a button on your earbuds? Why?

**Block C — breaks and coming back (42–52)**
14. Tell me about the **last time** you went several days without running. How long was it? How did you start again?
15. When you come back to a show, book or podcast after a gap, what do you usually do?
16. Do you remember where you left off in the last thing you listened to? What helps you remember?
17. Has missing a few runs ever made you stop running for a long time? What brought you back, if anything?

**Block D — what they already do (52–58)**
18. Have you ever paid for, or spent time on, something to help you keep running (apps, gear, a club, a class)? When was the last time?
19. What do you do now to get yourself out the door?

## 6. Commitment asks (only at the end, minute 58 or later)
Give a one-sentence description only if they ask. Choose one or two asks and note what they give up (time, reputation, money). Compliments don't count.
- **Time:** "Next week I'll have a short sample. Would you run with it once and give me 15 minutes of feedback?"
- **Reputation:** "Is there a friend who runs that I could talk to? Could you introduce us?"
- **Follow-up:** "I'll write up what I learned. Would you review it and tell me what I got wrong?"

Record: **said yes with a date**, **maybe**, or **brushed off**. "Sure, let me know when it's ready" is a polite stall, not a commitment.

## 7. Repair moves during the interview
- **They compliment you** → don't accept it; go back to their behavior: "Thanks. How are you handling that today?"
- **They give fluff** ("I usually…", "I would…", "I might…") → anchor to a real event: "When was the last time that happened? Walk me through it."
- **They give an idea or request** → write it down, don't promise it, and ask why: "What would that let you do that you can't now?"
- **They show strong emotion** (annoyed, embarrassed, delighted) → always dig deeper before moving on.
- **You slipped into pitching** → say "Sorry, I started talking about my idea. Back to what you were saying…"

## 8. Note-taking
Record audio with consent and take short notes. Write **what they said or did**, using exact quotes in quotation marks; keep your interpretation in a separate column.

| Q | Quote or specific behavior | Signal | Goal (1/2/3) |
|---|---|---|---|

Signals: 😀 excited · 😠 annoyed · 😳 embarrassed · ⚡ pain · 🔧 workaround they already use · 🎯 their goal · 💰 money or budget.

## 9. After the five interviews (30-minute review)
1. List what each person actually did in the last run, last early stop and last break. Count patterns, e.g. "3 of 5".
2. Mark each goal: **confirmed** (4–5 of 5), **weakened** (2–3), **disproved** (0–1).
3. Pull the strongest quotes and the commitments that were really given.
4. Write down what surprised you, and choose the next 3 big questions.
5. Save the review as `docs/research/findings.md`.

- [ ] Did I write down "I would…" statements as facts?
- [ ] Do I have a specific "last time" story for each goal?
