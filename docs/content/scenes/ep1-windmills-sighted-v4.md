# Ep1 test scene: windmills sighted (voice test, v4 "character comedy")

**Source:** Ormsby 1885, Part I, ch. VIII. Events kept: windmills sighted, Sancho says windmills, Don Quixote says giants and scolds him, lance lowered, the charge begins. Everything else is new writing.
**Type:** core · **Length:** about 2 minutes (about 300 words) · **Voices:** 2 (Sancho also speaks the scene-setting)
**Rules used:** `../script-style-guide.md` §1 (comedy engine), `../adaptation-prompt.md`
**v3 → v4:** fewer one-liners, more character. Don Quixote now has quiet, serious moments and "wins" an argument; Sancho is earnest instead of witty; the runner is Sancho's accomplice; the bread chain (Bread → Destiny knocks → very good bread) is kept; the wind joke becomes the one real surprise; the "who pays for the windmill" gag is new; the scene ends on a hook. Briareus is kept out of this scene and saved for the charge scene.

## Plain text (captions)
**Sancho:** Friend. Picture the ridge ahead of you. Forty windmills, tall as cathedrals, their great wooden sails groaning round and round. Every time a sail crosses the sun, its shadow sweeps over us like the hand of a clock. A perfectly ordinary sight in the countryside. Unfortunately, my master has just seen them.
**Don Quixote:** Sancho.
**Sancho:** Oh, no.
**Don Quixote:** Do you see them?
**Sancho:** Yes.
**Don Quixote:** Giants.
**Sancho:** Windmills.
**Don Quixote:** Thirty giants! Perhaps a thousand! I did not count! Each with four enormous arms!
**Sancho:** Master. They grind flour. For bread. You like bread.
**Don Quixote:** Bread! Destiny knocks, and my squire answers with bread!
**Sancho:** It is very good bread.
**Don Quixote:** Sancho. The one on the left.
**Sancho:** The windmill?
**Don Quixote:** The giant. He has been watching us.
**Sancho:** He has no eyes.
**Don Quixote:** Precisely. A professional.
**Sancho:** Friend, you're still with me? Good. One of us should be.
**Don Quixote:** Look! He waves! He challenges me!
**Sancho:** That's the wind, master.
**Don Quixote:** And why is the wind blowing?
**Sancho:** Because... Actually, I don't know.
**Don Quixote:** Aha! Even you admit there is a mystery!
**Sancho:** He won. He actually won one.
**Sancho:** Master, before you attack. If you destroy it, who pays for the windmill?
**Don Quixote:** The king.
**Sancho:** Which king?
**Don Quixote:** We shall discuss that after the battle.
**Sancho:** Friend, he's doing the thing with the lance. Whatever happens, do not follow him. Stay with me. Far behind. The next county.
**Don Quixote:** For glory!
**Sancho:** I should have stayed home and eaten the bread.
**Sancho:** Oh. He's actually going. Friend... I think we are about to witness something very expensive.

## Sound layer (separate; add after the dialogue is generated)

A professional." · sail creak on "He waves" · lance lowered (metal scrape) on "doing the thing with the lance" · hoofbeats starting on "For glory!", reluctant at first · keep everything well under the dialogue; no sudden loud peaks.

## Safety check against the rules
- No pace, speed or effort remark about the runner. "You're still with me?" only checks the listener is paying attention to the story.
- "Do not follow him" is an in-story action, not a physical command.
- No line pushes the runner to continue.

## Listen for
- [ ] The first lines give a picture and a smile
- [ ] Don Quixote's quiet moments ("A professional.") get a laugh, not only the loud ones
- [ ] The chain Bread → Destiny knocks → very good bread still works
- [ ] "He actually won one" lands as the surprise
- [ ] "Who pays?" feels like a real worry, not a joke told by Sancho
- [ ] The scene ends with you wanting the next one
