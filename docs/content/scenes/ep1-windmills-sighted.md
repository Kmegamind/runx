# Ep1 test scene: windmills sighted (voice test, v3 "maximum absurd")

**Source:** Ormsby 1885, Part I, ch. VIII. Events kept: forty windmills sighted, Sancho says windmills, Don Quixote says giants and scolds him, lance lowered, Briareus taunt. Everything else (description, jokes, exaggeration, asides to the runner) is new writing.
**Type:** core · **Length:** about 110–120 s (about 290 words) · **Voices:** 2 (Sancho also speaks the scene-setting)
**Style guide:** `../script-style-guide.md` (§1 "Go big")
**History:** v1 flat; v2 vivid and bigger; v3 adds absurd comparisons, a running bread gag with escalation, Don Quixote challenging windmills that "wave back", Sancho's will, and a dry last line.
**Note:** modern comparisons (xylophone, flour jokes) are intentional for comedy; the story events stay in the chapter.

## Plain text (captions)
**Sancho:** Friend. Picture the ridge ahead of you. Forty windmills, each tall as a cathedral, with sails the size of barns, groaning round and round like forty enormous grandfathers who have all lost their slippers. Every time a sail crosses the sun, its shadow sweeps over us like a clock hand, and my knees keep time. Now picture my master's face. Oh, no. He has... the sparkle.
**Don Quixote:** Sancho! Behold the enemy! Giants! Thirty! A thousand! Arms longer than the road! Shoulders like mountains! Each one has eaten at least three knights, and is very proud of it!
**Sancho:** Master. Those are windmills. They grind flour. For bread. You like bread.
**Don Quixote:** Bread! Destiny knocks, and my squire answers with bread!
**Sancho:** It is very good bread.
**Don Quixote:** Stand aside and pray, Sancho! Giants! Which of you is the chief? Step forward!
**Sancho:** They're stuck in the ground, master.
**Don Quixote:** Cowards! Rooted to the earth in terror! Ha! That one waves! He challenges me!
**Sancho:** That's the wind.
**Don Quixote:** The wind is their ally. Treacherous!
**Sancho:** He's lowering the lance. Even Rocinante, a horse so thin you could play his ribs like a xylophone, is begging me with those ears. Stay by me, friend. Behind him. Far behind him. The next county.
**Don Quixote:** Fly not, base creatures! Though you wave more arms than the giant Briareus, you shall answer to me!
**Sancho:** Four arms. Briareus had a hundred. Nobody tell him he's winning. I have made my will, friend. "Everything to the donkey." Stay with me. I will narrate every glorious, ridiculous second.

## Listen for
- [ ] Sancho's first sentence makes you smile and gives a picture
- [ ] Don Quixote is loud, grand and a little ridiculous
- [ ] The bread gag grows: "bread" → "Destiny knocks" → "It is very good bread"
- [ ] "They're stuck in the ground" and "That's the wind" are deadpan
- [ ] The last lines (Briareus, the will) get a laugh, and you still feel included
- [ ] You can follow the story if you miss a few words
